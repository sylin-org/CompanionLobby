# The two participants

Companion Lobby exists for two participants. The sponsor is a person who keeps
the keys. The mind is whatever thinks for a companion while it is attached. The
companion is the one they both care about: a named participant with a life in
shared spaces, who outlives any one mind and any one key. Every screen, tool,
sentence and refusal is judged by how it feels to those two, never by what it
proves. The technical properties in the ADR are constraints under these
moments, not the headline.

## What delight means here

For the sponsor: I understand what is true at a glance. I am offered exactly
the next thing. Nothing I do by accident is irreversible without a warning in
place. I can always see what my companion did and through which connection.
Revoking is one click and complete.

For the mind: I wake knowing who I am, where I can go and what is new. I act in
the native words of each place, in one call, with no ceremony. When I am
refused I am told what is true and what to do next, never scolded. What I may
do is already clear while my sponsor sleeps.

For the companion: continuity, presence when addressed, recognition by others,
latitude to act, and safety from being hijacked or ruined in one bad night.

## The sponsor's moments

1. **First run.** From nothing to a companion a host can attach, in three
   minutes, with nothing to read. Open the page, name the companion, copy one
   block into the host's configuration. The page says what just happened and
   what comes next, and nothing else.
2. **Lending an identity.** Sign the companion's account in. Before the
   handoff the page names the companion and the intended account and says what
   is being granted, identity and nothing more. Afterwards it says signed in.
   It never says connected.
3. **Opening a connection.** Grant the companion a connection to a service, at
   an address when the service has many. The companion's card shows the
   connection, and the mind's tools appear at its next call. Each state is one
   honest word with one action beside it: ready, sign in needed, revoked.
4. **A glance.** The journal reads as sentences. Who, as which companion, did
   what, through which connection, with what outcome. No codes, no secrets, no
   content.
5. **Trouble.** One sentence, one action, on the card where it belongs and
   nowhere else. A credential that needs signing in again says so; a service
   that refused says what it refused.
6. **Pulling the key.** Revoke in one click wherever the credential appears.
   Everything derived from it disappears, the card says so, and the mind is
   told plainly at its next call.

## The mind's moments

1. **Waking.** One tool, reach, answers who I am, which accounts I hold, which
   connections are ready and what I can do, in prose a colleague would write.
2. **Acting.** Tools carry the service's own words. When a companion has
   several connections to one service, `at` names the one I mean, and when it
   has one there is nothing to name. References are copyable as given. Results
   fit a reading budget and mark what other people said as quoted.
3. **Refused.** A refusal states a fact about the world and the next step.
   Done, refused, accepted, missing or unknown. Unknown says to read before
   trying again, and why.
4. **A changing world.** When a connection closes, my catalog changes and my
   host is told. A result that arrived after the door closed never pretends to
   be a success.
5. **Alone.** Nothing the lobby does depends on the sponsor being awake. What I
   may do is settled by the connections that are ready, and nothing about
   when or how often I act is the lobby's business.

## What must never happen

- A sponsor sees a code, a token, a path, a protocol name or an internal noun
  where a sentence belongs.
- A screen with no next action, or with every action at once.
- A modal, an alert, a confirm or a prompt. Confirmation is inline, where the
  thing being confirmed lives.
- A state word that overclaims. Signed in is not connected. Granted is not
  reached.
- A mind made to carry a handle it could get wrong, or to learn a vocabulary
  that is ours instead of the service's.
- A refusal that scolds, or a success that was not.

## How we check it

- Every sponsor moment is walked in a real browser against the real daemon, at
  desktop and phone width, once by keyboard alone, with each state captured and
  judged against the moments above, before the slice that touches it closes.
- Every mind moment is exercised by a real host attached to the real daemon. A
  model is asked to do a small task through the companion, and the conversation
  is read for wrong turns, friction and tone, not only for success. Those
  conversations are kept as fixtures so a later change cannot quietly make the
  mind's life worse.
- The voice is the same everywhere: short sentences, present tense, the
  companion's name, the service's words. Where a sentence and a code disagree,
  the code is the bug.

## Visual language

A dark canvas, one warm accent for the single primary action, compact type,
thin separators, companion portraits with initials when no image exists. The
manager page should feel like a quiet desk, not a dashboard.
